Google Search and the end Of Choice (and why DuckDuckGo is not the answer)

11.06.2026

So Google decided to push AI Overview above everything else. This is not a “Search Engine” anymore, this is now a full-on chatbot experience, and to people like me, who grew with the web and saw the evolution from Altavista to IE to Google, changing search results for an AI overview is almost an abomination.

Why? I believe it's choice. I'd rather take this source over that one; I'd rather go with text than with video, and so on. Searching the web was that special skill that we had that gave us choice.

But choice has changed its meaning over time. You certainly remember this ad that aged rather poorly:

We had listings. Then ads arrived. They were initially (relatively) unobtrusive, but began to become more prominent over the years. Then they were moved above the fold, so the most likely thing to click was an ad. That's pure manipulation, but we got used to scrolling down a few lines to see where the real search results started if we didn't care for ads.

Ads became more and more prominent, but they were still labeled as ads. The annoy was there, but we knew what we were dealing with. At one point, we would use Google to search for the official domain of our banking websites, our schools, city services, essentially everything. The URL bar became the entry point of our digital life and there was no escape.

Enter the AI Overview

Today, I don't even know if what I'm seeing is an ad, a hallucination of the model, or a genuine answer, collected from millions of websites who unknowingly contributed their content to feed the AI.

The first link takes me to a paid app, and the second one is broken. Nothing wrong with paid apps, but not knowing if what I was seeing was the result of a paid campaign or simply the summary of what I was anyway going to find below scared me.

For this specific thing I was looking for, I knew it could be done differently, so I politely declined what Google was kindly offering.

Embracing DuckDuckGo or maybe not?

So I wented to DuckDuckGo. A for-profit that sells itself as user-centric, and it provided a different result, also using AI, also linking to a very old, unmaintained package that was probably deprecated (the #2 in Google's AI overview).

Not very satisfactory. AI overview's aside, I think that copying Google's model is just plain wrong, @DuckDuckGo, don't go there.

Plain ol' search yields results, people

So I went back to Google, but this time I scrolled down to the actual search results. I found a more decent alternative to my problem via StackExchange (which is the thing I would have done a few years ago), just looking through answers by hand, as in the old times.

I found the solution, linked in a comment; more exactly buried in a comment.

Now, I'm really concerned for people. Having just one decent search engine was already pretty bad for many reasons, but when that search engine decides to stop being a search engine and becomes something else, society is at risk. Students, kids, people who won't scroll past the first page, people who will gladly take whatever is offered over something that's genuinely, objectively better, just because they weren't trained in the art of finding the gems beneath the slop. And there's not even a way to symbolically contribute back by liking the original comment, that's gone.

Here's the package for the curious minds: https://github.com/sbarex/SourceCodeSyntaxHighlight

or simply do

brew install syntax-highlight

(and then open the app at least once for highlighting to take effect)

The monopoly of knowledge

We have arrived in an era in which knowledge does not belong to everybody. It never has, of course, but these times are worse than ever as the tools we have are not only giving us bad choices: they are depriving us of them. They are void of transparency, and they are not even linking back to the original creators so they at least get an incentive to keep producing content.

And this is only the beginning (or the end?).

We need to do something

I have a few ideas: I imagine that LLMs will allow us to create different interfaces for the different needs that we have, but search is one that's genuinely hard and nobody seems to be taking it seriously (or the problem is so difficult that nobody has succeeded yet).

Here are some of my very rough ideas to fix this:

  • Revive RSS: old technologies that privilege content from trusted, curated sources
  • Use LLMs (local) to translate intent and tweak results based on personas
  • Use LLMs to find the hidden gems, iteratively. This would be too expensive for Search Engines to do on your behalf, and because their incentives wouldn't be aligned with yours, but it's something that might be done locally if participant websites are willing to provide structured content.
  • Build indexes of semantic searches, with people reporting back what worked (only trusted communities). Hard to achieve and to police.

I might start working on this, if my search results keep declining and especially, if the overview keeps hiding the good stuff from me.

But for now I have to get back to my YAML files after this little detour, they are waiting for me!
